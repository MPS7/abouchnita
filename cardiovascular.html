RESEARCH FOCUS

From cellular activation to clot formation under flow.

My cardiovascular research links blood flow, transport, biochemical coagulation reactions, and platelet signalling to explain clot initiation and growth. I develop models that examine how clot architecture and pathway inhibition depend on local biophysical conditions.

GUIDING QUESTIONS
  • When does a localized injury trigger sustained coagulation?
  • How do transport and platelet signaling shape the growing clot?
  • Which mechanisms may explain variable effects of pathway inhibition?

STUDY 01 / 2024 · Platelet signalling and thrombus organization

Intracellular calcium regulation shapes thrombus formation and response to pathway inhibition

My collaborators and I developed a multiscale description of platelet accumulation under flow that links transport and local agonist concentrations to intracellular calcium activation. The model captures how activated platelets recruit additional cells and develop spatially heterogeneous thrombi.

Spatially varying signalling helps explain the appearance of more strongly activated platelet regions and a surrounding shell. Simulated interference with ADP/P2Y- and thrombin/PAR-related activation altered clot growth and composition, with effects depending on how the pathways contribute to recruitment and stability.

These results provide hypotheses about where and when platelet-signalling pathways become limiting. They are simulation-based mechanistic findings, not estimates of clinical treatment efficacy.

Bouchnita & Volpert · Computation · 2024 ↗

STUDY 02 / 2020 · Coagulation initiation in flowing blood

Shear rate, platelet count, and injury geometry jointly determine whether coagulation initiates

With collaborators, I developed a spatial reaction–transport model coupling coagulation chemistry to blood flow near a localized vessel-wall injury. Varying shear rate, platelet count, and injury size allowed us to examine the conditions for sufficient thrombin accumulation to initiate coagulation.

The calculations revealed threshold-like initiation behavior resulting from the balance between local biochemical production and convective removal. The implication is that clot initiation is controlled by the interaction of chemistry, flow, and vessel geometry, rather than any one factor in isolation.

Bouchnita et al. · PLOS ONE · 2020 ↗
