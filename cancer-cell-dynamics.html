Cancer & Cell Dynamics

RESEARCH FOCUS

From molecular signaling to heterogeneous tumor growth.

My cancer research connects receptor signalling, intracellular regulation, tumor-cell phenotypes, and the effects of treatment or immune responses. I am particularly interested in how heterogeneity among individual cells gives rise to tumor-level behavior.

GUIDING QUESTIONS
  • How do changes in receptor signaling affect collective growth?
  • How do dormant and proliferative states alter immune surveillance?
  • How do heterogeneous tumor populations respond to therapy?

STUDY 01 / 2026 · Phenotype-structured tumor–immune dynamics

ERK/p38-regulated dormancy links cellular state to tumor–immune outcomes

In a study with my collaborators, we represented tumor cells along a continuous ERK/p38-regulated phenotype axis rather than as two fixed dormant and proliferating states. The model connects cellular heterogeneity to tumor growth, epidermal growth factor, and cytotoxic immune responses.

Comparison with published p38-inhibition experiments connected changes in dormancy regulation to growth trajectories. The same framework showed how immune pressure and phenotype transitions can produce elimination, equilibrium, escape, or dormant-escape dynamics without changing the model into separate disease-specific cases.

Together, these results identify the distribution of tumor states—rather than tumor size alone—as an important determinant of immune surveillance and subsequent growth. These are mechanistic predictions that motivate further experimental study.

Baffoe & Bouchnita · Frontiers in Immunology · 2026 ↗

STUDY 02 / 2019 · Molecular signalling to three-dimensional growth

Receptor-scale mechanisms shape multicellular tumor development

In earlier work with collaborators, I developed a three-dimensional multiscale model linking stochastic ligand–receptor interactions, intracellular EGFR/ERK signalling, and the behavior of individual tumor cells. This coupling allowed molecular perturbations to influence cellular decisions and simulated tissue-scale growth.

Simulations investigated the consequences of EGFR overexpression and alterations in the Ras/Raf pathway. The results supported a mechanistic explanation of how increased signalling activity can accelerate tumor growth, demonstrating why a fixed phenomenological growth rate may miss the effects of molecular perturbations.

Bouchnita, Hellander & Hellander · Bulletin of Mathematical Biology · 2019 ↗
