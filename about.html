ABOUT

Building explanatory and predictive models of living systems.

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematical Sciences at The University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP). I lead an interdisciplinary research program connecting mathematical modelling, computational science, and biomedical questions.

My research connects molecular signalling, cell behaviour, and tissue-scale processes to disease outcomes and population health. I have worked on tumor growth and drug resistance, platelet activation and thrombosis, viral–immune interactions, respiratory-disease forecasting, and mechanistically informed machine learning.

Academic leadership and service

I serve as Co-Director Extension of the Data Science Ph.D. Program at UTEP and as an Associate Editor of Mathematical Modelling of Natural Phenomena (MMNP). I am also a Co-Investigator in epiENGAGE, a multi-institutional initiative in outbreak modelling and public-health decision support.

Training and research trajectory

My training includes two Ph.D. degrees, in mathematical modelling and scientific computing and in physiology and organismal biology. Before joining UTEP in 2022, I held postdoctoral positions at Uppsala University and the UT Austin COVID-19 Modeling Consortium, where I worked on multiscale methods and infectious-disease modelling with interdisciplinary teams.

Scientific direction

My current goal is to develop methods that combine mechanistic models with AI-based inference, particularly when biological observations are sparse or incomplete. Working with students and collaborators, I use these methods to test hypotheses, assess uncertainty, and identify measurements that can distinguish competing biological explanations.
