RESEARCH

Developing multiscale models.
Learning from biological observations.

My research program develops multiscale mathematical models, numerical methods, and mechanistically informed AI to connect biological processes across scales, extract information from limited measurements, and explain disease dynamics, treatment response, and population outcomes.

RESEARCH PROGRAM

Methods that connect mechanisms, observations and inference.

From molecular mechanisms to population-level outcomes

I develop mathematical and computational methods for biological systems in which processes across spatial and temporal scales interact. Molecular reactions regulate individual cells, cellular interactions influence tissue organization, and physiological, immunological, or behavioral differences shape outcomes within individuals and across communities.

My approach brings mechanistic models and AI-based inference together rather than treating them as separate tools. My collaborators and I use equations and biological assumptions to guide what can be learned from experimental, clinical, and epidemiological data—especially when direct measurements are incomplete.

01 · MODEL DEVELOPMENT

Mechanisms and interacting scales

My group develops ordinary and partial differential equation models, individual-based and stochastic simulations, and coupled reaction–transport descriptions. These formulations connect receptor signalling, cellular transitions, tissue transport, immune regulation, and contact-driven transmission.

02 · COMPUTATIONAL METHODS

Numerical simulation and virtual experiments

I work with students and collaborators to develop computational simulations that resolve heterogeneity, explore uncertain parameters, and compare interventions under controlled conditions. Virtual experiments help identify mechanisms that are difficult to separate experimentally.

03 · INFERENCE AND LEARNING

Mechanistically informed AI

In my research, detailed mechanistic simulations can supply training data for computationally efficient surrogate models, while known biological relationships constrain inference from sparse observations. I study identifiability, latent states, uncertainty, and the predicted effects of interventions.

04 · BIOLOGICAL INSIGHT

Testing hypotheses across domains

My group applies these methods to cancer progression and treatment resistance, platelet activation and thrombosis, viral infection and immunity, and physiological or community health. Across these settings, I seek testable explanations for heterogeneous outcomes and the mechanisms that regulate them.

A common methodological foundation, distinct biological questions.

In cancer, my work connects intracellular regulation and heterogeneous phenotypes to growth and therapy response. In cardiovascular systems, I study how platelet signalling and blood flow influence clot formation. In infectious diseases, I investigate viral replication, immunity, and epidemic trajectories. In population health, my current research examines physiological and behavioural heterogeneity and its implications for interventions. Each application retains its own biological assumptions and evidence.

ONE METHODOLOGY · SEVERAL BIOLOGICAL SYSTEMS

Biological questions enabled by the methods.

I adapt a shared methodological foundation to distinct biological questions, with each application grounded in its own data and mechanisms.

The sections below connect selected published findings with the papers, methods, and biological questions behind them.

01 / CANCER & CELL DYNAMICS

How hidden cell states control growth and response

My cancer studies connect EGFR signalling and cell interactions to tumor growth, and use structured population models to examine heterogeneity, resistance, and dormancy. In a 2026 study with a student collaborator, an ERK/p38 phenotype helped explain changes in tumor–immune elimination and escape.

Bouchnita et al., Bull. Math. Biol., 2019 ↗ · Baffoe & Bouchnita, Front. Immunol., 2026 ↗

02 / CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEMS

How flow, biochemical reactions and cell activation form a clot

Coupled hemodynamics, coagulation chemistry and platelet-level calcium dynamics explain conditions under which clots initiate and develop a core–shell structure. Mechanistic simulations have also enabled virtual-patient datasets for efficient, interpretable anticoagulation-response prediction.

Bouchnita & Volpert, Computation, 2024 ↗ · Bouchnita et al., Math. Biosci., 2022 ↗

03 / INFECTION & IMMUNITY

How changing immunity shapes infections and epidemics

Viral kinetics and structured immunity models connect intracellular antiviral responses, population susceptibility and variant emergence. Forecasting and scenario analyses quantify the potential impact of vaccination and other interventions as the underlying immune landscape evolves.

Bouchnita & Volpert, J. Theor. Biol., 2026 ↗ · Bouchnita et al., Epidemics, 2024 ↗

04 / POPULATION, BEHAVIOR & HEALTH

How individual differences may alter community outcomes

My ongoing work examines physiological and behavioural heterogeneity, including stress, activity, school environments, and intervention participation. The aim is to understand how individual differences may affect responses to community interventions.

Current methodological direction; findings from these ongoing applications are not presented as published validation.

MECHANISTIC AI & INFERENCE

Learning without abandoning mechanisms.

Detailed simulations resolve processes that cannot routinely be measured, but can be expensive at population scale. I therefore develop and use efficient learning methods constrained by mechanistic structure and available observations. Previous published examples include EGFR/ERK signalling and individualized clotting simulations; ongoing work extends the approach to hidden-state inference from sparse biomedical data.
